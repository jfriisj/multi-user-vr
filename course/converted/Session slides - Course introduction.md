Automating the installation of cables
Challenge:
Many industrial and domestic products require the installation of wires, cables
and harnesses. The robotic manipulation of flexible objects such as cables is
challenging, as the object shape is not known exactly after grasping. Novel
computer vision algorithms such as TrackDLO allow pose estimation of
flexible objects (specifically Deformable Linear Objects (DLO)) and simulation
software such as MuJoCo supports simulation of flexible objects.
Consequently, learning-based policies for the manipulation of flexible objects
can be developed based on state-of-the-art methods. Robotic manipulation of
flexible objects may enable automation of tasks performed solely by humans
today. This opens the possibility for new businesses within this field of
application.

---

Automating the installation of cables
Challenge:
Autonomous mobile robots often use 2D or 3D lidar sensors for obstacle
detection and navigation due to their precision and robustness in a variety of
lighting conditions. However, lidar alone does not provide semantic
understanding — it can detect that "something" is there, but not what that
"something" is. RGB cameras, in contrast, are well-suited for object
classification and semantic segmentation, but lack accurate depth
information. Combining these two sensor types offers a promising way to
enrich lidar data with meaningful object labels.
In real-world industrial settings, the robot frequently encounters semi-static
objects such as chairs, pallets, bins, or carts — items that may move
occasionally but often remain in place for hours or days. These objects should
be handled differently than static structures like walls or pillars, which are
used for localization. Without semantic understanding, robots may mistakenly
treat all lidar returns as either static or dynamic, potentially corrupting maps or
causing unnecessary avoidance behavior.

Automating the straightening of clothes
Challenge:
Straightening/folding clothes is an everyday domestic as well as industrial
task. The robotic manipulation of flexible objects such as clothes is
challenging, as the object shape is not known exactly after grasping. Artificial
Intelligence models such as OpenVLA and startups like Physical Intelligence
are demonstrating significant potential for robots to be able to perform such
complicated tasks. Consequently, learning-based policies for the manipulation
of flexible objects can be developed based on state-of-the-art methods.
Robotic manipulation of flexible objects may enable automation of tasks
performed solely by humans today. This opens the possibility for new
businesses within this field of application.
