
## User research

### 1. "What's the Point?" (Lean Startup Philosophy)

The core philosophy is that "7 out of 10 new products fail because nobody wants them". To avoid this, you must step into your customer's shoes and test your assumptions through interviews rather than surveys.

* **Goal:** Move from "assumptions" to "validated facts" about the problem you are solving.
* **The Process:** Define a persona, map their profile, and create a research plan to interview potential customers.

### 2. "The Customer Profile" (The Value Proposition Canvas)

This is a tool used to map out the human side of your business model. It consists of three specific areas:

* **Customer Jobs:** The tasks your users are trying to perform, the problems they are trying to solve, or the needs they are trying to satisfy.
* **Pains:** The negative emotions, risks, and obstacles your customers face before, during, and after getting the job done.
* **Gains:** The benefits and positive outcomes your customers expect or desire from a solution.

### 3. "Practical User Research" (The EIS Report)

Your **EIS User Research** report applies these theories to the medical training domain. Through interviews with trainees and specialists (like Jacob, Simone, and Sebastian), you identified critical gaps:

* **The Gap:** Existing workshops lack the realism and multi-person stress exposure needed for high-pressure decision-making.
* **The Prioritized Problems:** You found that **role/ownership ambiguity** and a **lack of practical decision aids** (like checklists) are the most significant pain points for medical staff.
* **Personal Stress:** Trainees often fear personal performance failure ("not to f*** up"), which hinders decisive action.

* **Problem Hypothesis:** Your assumption about what pain point the user has before you validate it with interviews.

## Team Composition & Business Model

### 1. "What's the point?" (Purpose & Context)

The goal of these documents is to ensure that a technically sound product actually has a viable business behind it and a team capable of delivering it. It addresses the fact that most startups fail not because the tech is broken, but because there is no market for it or the team cannot work together.

* 
**Focus on:** The transition from a "product" (the VR system) to a "business" (the Lean Canvas).


* **Ask yourself:** "If our VR system is the engine, who is the driver, where are we going, and how do we pay for the gas?"

### 2. "The 3 Key Takeaways" (Main Results)

* 
**Balanced Teams Succeed:** Founder teams that balance a "technical nerd" (product focus) with a "visionary" (business focus) raise 30% more money and grow their customer base significantly faster. You must map both professional skills (coding, hardware) and personal traits.


* 
**The DISC Behavioral Framework:** Understanding the four main behavioral types—**D**ominance (results), **I**nfluence (ideas/relationships), **S**teadiness (stability/community), and **C**ompliance (facts/logic)—is used to improve team communication and resolve conflicts.


* 
**Lean Canvas Validation:** Instead of a long business plan, the Lean Canvas forces you to identify the **Problem**, **Solution**, and **Unfair Advantage** (something that cannot be easily copied, like academic expertise or a unique team).



### 3. "The Analogy Method" (Understanding)

* 
**The Team as an Orchestra:** You cannot have a band made entirely of lead singers (D and I types). You need the "rhythm section" (S and C types) to keep the tempo, handle the details, and ensure the music (the project) is accurate and on time.


* 
**Lean Canvas as a Survival Map:** A traditional business plan is like a massive textbook; a Lean Canvas is a one-page map that shows you exactly where the food is (revenue) and where the traps are (costs and competitors) so you can survive the first 6 months.



### 4. "The Connection" (The Red Thread)

* 
**The Connection:** Your technical paper proves the Meta Quest 3 system is **feasible** (it works). The business documents prove the project is **viable** (it has a team and a plan). The user research (EIS report) identified the **Problem**, and the Lean Canvas turns that problem into a **Revenue Stream**.

## Creating pitch 

### 1. "What's the Point?" (Purpose & Context)

The purpose of a pitch is not to explain every technical detail of your project, but to **convince an investor** that you have a "perfect solution," the "timing is right," and an "incredible team". It is a high-level summary designed for live meetings to spark interest and secure a follow-up.

* **The Goal**: To present a compelling business case where the "benefit is enormous" for the customers.
* **The Focus**: Focus on the language of the presentation and use the front page as a strong summary to add value during Q&A sessions.

### 2. "The 3 Key Takeaways" (Main Components)

* **The Problem & Solution Gap**: A great pitch starts with a clear "problem/market opportunity" and shows why your solution is the perfect fit.
* **The Financial Case**: You need a sharp understanding of the business case, including revenue, gross margin, and earnings. Investors value the **assumptions** behind your numbers more than the accuracy of the decimals.
* **The "Deal"**: You must clearly state the **Funding** requirements—how much capital you need, what milestones you will hit, and what specific skills you are looking for in an investor.

### 3. "The Analogy Method" (Understanding)

* **The Pitch as a Movie Trailer**: A pitch is like a **movie trailer**. It doesn't show you the whole movie (the technical code or full business plan), but it highlights the most exciting parts, the main characters (the team), and the big conflict (the problem) so that the audience (the investors) wants to buy a ticket to see more.

### 4. "The Connection" (The Red Thread)

* **The Connection**: Your technical paper proves the system is **stable**. Your user research proves the problem is **real**. The **Pitch Template** is the final step where you package that stability and reality into a "deal" for an investor. It takes the **Unique Value Proposition** from your Lean Canvas and turns it into a persuasive story for the market.

---

### 1. "What's the Point?" (Purpose & Context)

The paper investigates how various digital technologies (DTs) collectively support the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). The authors argue that while technology is a powerful tool for solving global issues like poverty and climate change, many products fail because they don't address real human needs or face significant adoption barriers. The goal is to provide a "blueprint" for leaders to use technology in a way that is ethical and inclusive.

### 2. "The 3 Key Takeaways" (Main Results)

* **Technologies are Specific "Solvers":** Different tools tackle specific goals. For example, **AI** improves healthcare diagnostics (SDG 3), **Blockchain** creates transparent food supply chains to fight hunger (SDG 2), and **IoT** sensors make cities more energy-efficient (SDG 11).


* **Real-Time Optimization:** These technologies allow for "data-driven" decisions. By using real-time monitoring, governments and businesses can manage natural resources better and deliver services like education more effectively to remote areas.


* 
**The "Digital Divide" is the Biggest Barrier:** The transformative power of tech is limited by unequal access to the internet, high costs, and a lack of digital skills in developing regions.

The "Red Thread" here is **Inclusivity and Feasibility**.

* **Context:** This paper provides the big-picture "Why" for your technical work.
* 
**Link to your VR Project:** Your project's technical stability (low latency and high frame rate) is exactly what is needed to make digital training "inclusive". By proving your system works on affordable Quest 3 hardware, you are helping bridge the **"Digital Divide"** by making professional-grade training (SDG 4: Quality Education) accessible to more people without the need for expensive, high-end rigs.

**"Robotics: Enabler and inhibitor of the Sustainable Development Goals,"** 
provides a multidisciplinary assessment of how the global "megatrend" of robotics impacts the United Nations' 2030 agenda.

---

### 1. "What’s the Point?" (Purpose & Context)

The paper aims to fill a gap in scientific literature by specifically evaluating **robotics** (physical machines with sense-think-act capabilities) rather than just software-based Artificial Intelligence. The researchers used a consensus-based expert elicitation process to analyze all **17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)** and their **169 Targets** to determine if current robotic technology helps (enables) or hinders (inhibits) their achievement.

### 2. "The 3 Key Takeaways" (Main Results)

* 
**High Enabling Potential:** Robotics has the potential to enable **46% of the SDG Targets**, with the strongest positive impacts seen in industrial productivity and environmental protection.


* 
**Significant Inhibiting Risks:** Conversely, robotics could inhibit **19% of the Targets**, primarily by exacerbating social inequalities and creating tensions in labor markets.


* 
**Double-Edged Tool:** While generally having a positive influence, robotics is a "double-edged tool" that can automate dangerous tasks (like mine clearing or hazardous waste handling) but may also create "e-waste" and increase income gaps between skilled and unskilled workers.



### 3. "The Analogy Method" (Understanding)

Think of robotics as a **"Physical Power-Up"** for the Sustainable Development Goals:

* Imagine the 17 SDGs are a set of complex construction projects needed to fix the planet.
* Standard digital technology (like AI) is the **blueprint** and the **calculator** used to plan the work.
* 
**Robotics** is the **power tool** (the drill or the crane) that actually goes into the physical world to move the dirt, plant the seeds, or perform the surgery.


* Just like a power tool, if used correctly, it finishes the job faster and safer; if used poorly, it can break the materials or replace the workers who needed the job.

### 4. "The Connection" (The Red Thread)

The "Red Thread" here is **Social Responsibility and Accountability**.

* **Context:** This paper provides the ethical "guardrails" for technical development.
* 
**Link to your VR Project:** While your technical paper proves that a multi-user system is **feasible** and **stable**, this paper asks if that stability is being used to **empower** people (like paramedic training in SDG 3 or education in SDG 4) or if it risks excluding them due to high costs and the "digital divide".

**"Human factors and ethics in technology,"**
provides the philosophical and psychological framework for understanding why technology succeeds or fails based on human behavior and values.

---

### 1. "What's the Point?" (Purpose & Context)

The main point is that **human factors**—such as ethics, aesthetics, and convenience—are not side issues to be outsourced; they are central constraints and aids to successful design. Technology is not just about engineering; it is about how it interacts with human moral psychology and daily habits.

* 
**The Goal:** To integrate human-centered values directly into the design process to prevent costly later modifications.


* 
**The Core Question:** "Even if we *can* build it, will humans actually use it, and *should* we build it?".



### 2. "The 3 Key Takeaways" (Main Results)

* 
**Behavior over Adaptation:** It is often easier and less costly to change a user's expectations or behavior than it is to adapt a complex technology to existing flawed behaviors.


* 
**The Limits of TAM:** The **Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)** suggests people use tech based on "perceived usefulness" and "ease of use". However, this model is limited because it ignores fundamental values and the impact of habituation.


* 
**Ethics as a Design Tool:** Ethical theories like **Utilitarianism** (focus on consequences) and **Deontology** (focus on duties/rules) should be used as practical tools to assess the impact of a product on human wellbeing.



### 3. "The Analogy Method" (Understanding)

Think of Human Factors as **"The Shoes of Technology."**

* You can design the most advanced, high-tech running shoes in the world, but if they are the wrong size for the runner or look so ugly that the runner is embarrassed to wear them, the shoes are useless.
* "Human Factors" is the process of measuring the runner's foot (user research) and understanding their style (aesthetics and ethics) before you start manufacturing the shoe (engineering).



### 4. "The Connection" (The Red Thread)

This document provides the **"Human Logic"** that must be paired with your project's **"Technical Logic."**

* **Context:** Your technical paper proves the Quest 3 system is stable and precise.
* **The Link:** This ethics document explains *why* that precision matters. If your system has high latency, it causes a "pain" (cybersickness) that violates the human factor of "wellbeing". If your spatial calibration is off, it fails the "perceived ease of use" requirement of the TAM model, leading to zero adoption.
